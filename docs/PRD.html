2Executive Summary

One paragraph on the problem, the product, and the definition of done.

The store currently sells electronics through walk-in traffic and phone or messaging orders. Every order costs staff time to take down manually, stock levels live in someone's head, and the catalogue is invisible to anyone not already standing in the shop. This project replaces that with a public web storefront where customers browse a searchable product catalogue, read specifications and reviews, add items to a cart, and check out with either Cash on Delivery or an online payment — and a private admin dashboard where the owner manages products, stock, coupons, and the order pipeline without developer involvement.

The system is deliberately single-vendor: there is no seller onboarding, no commission logic, no vendor payouts. That constraint is what makes a six-week build plausible for one developer. Scope is one storefront, one inventory, one owner.

Definition of done for v1: a customer can find a product, order it, and pay for it; the owner can see that order, mark it shipped, and watch stock decrement — all through the web app, with no manual database edits and no developer in the loop.

2.1 What v1 includes

  • Product catalogue with categories, brands, and variants
  • Search, faceted filtering, and sorting
  • Cart with guest support and login merge
  • Checkout with Bangladesh address capture
  • COD and SSLCommerz online payment
  • Order history and status tracking
  • Customer accounts and address book
  • Reviews and ratings with verified-purchase badge
  • Wishlist
  • Coupon and discount engine
  • Inventory tracking with low-stock alerts
  • Shipping zones and configurable tax
  • Custom React admin dashboard
  • Transactional email notifications

3Goals & Success Metrics

What the business is buying with this build, and how it will know the build worked.

3.1 Business goals

GoalRationale
Sell outside the shop's catchment areaWalk-in traffic caps revenue at physical footfall. A storefront plus courier delivery removes the geographic ceiling.
Cut manual order-taking overheadPhone and messaging orders consume staff hours and produce transcription errors. Self-service checkout removes both.
Make stock levels truthfulOverselling an out-of-stock item damages trust and triggers refunds. A single inventory source of truth prevents it.
Build a repeat-customer channelAccounts, order history, and email give the store a way to reach past buyers that a walk-in shop does not have.
Give the owner operational controlPrice changes, new products, and promotions must not require a developer.

3.2 Product goals

  • A returning customer who knows what they want can go from landing page to placed order in under three minutes on a phone.
  • A new customer can judge whether a product suits them from the product page alone — specs, images, warranty, and reviews all present without leaving the page.
  • The owner can fulfil a complete order — see it, confirm it, mark it packed and shipped — in under 60 seconds in the admin dashboard.
  • Adding a new product with images, specs, variants, and stock takes under 5 minutes.

3.3 Success metrics

Baselines are unavailable pre-launch, so the first 60 days after go-live establish them. Targets below are the review thresholds for the 90-day check-in.

MetricTarget (90 days)Why it matters
Checkout conversion rate≥ 1.5% of sessionsThe headline health number for a new storefront.
Cart abandonment≤ 70%High abandonment points at checkout friction or surprise shipping cost.
Average order valueEstablish baseline; track trendTells whether coupons and related-product placement are earning their keep.
COD refusal / failed-delivery rate≤ 10% of COD ordersCOD refusals are a direct loss — courier cost with no revenue. High rates justify order confirmation calls or a COD cap.
Share of orders paid online≥ 25%Prepaid orders carry no refusal risk. Moving customers off COD is a real margin lever.
Admin time per order≤ 60 secondsDirectly measures whether the dashboard replaced manual work.
Oversell incidentsZeroAny occurrence is an inventory-logic bug, not a business fluctuation.
Product-page bounce rate≤ 55%Proxy for whether product data is complete enough to decide on.

3.4 Non-goals

Named explicitly so scope creep has to argue against a written line:

  • This is not a marketplace. No second seller, ever, in v1.
  • This is not a POS or accounting system. It does not replace in-shop billing or produce financial statements.
  • This is not a warehouse management system. Stock is a number per variant, not bin locations and pick paths.
  • No native mobile app. The web app is mobile-first and that is the mobile strategy for v1.

4Personas & User Stories

Who uses the system, what they are trying to accomplish, and how success is judged.

4.1 Personas

PersonaProfilePrimary needs
Shopper
External, unauthenticated or logged in		 Price-comparing electronics buyer, largely on an Android phone over mobile data. Cautious about paying online before seeing goods. Trustworthy specs, real photos, honest stock status, clear delivery cost and time, and the option to pay cash on delivery.
Store Admin
Internal, owner/manager		 Owner or manager. Comfortable with a browser, not with a terminal or a database client. Add and price products fast, see today's orders at a glance, move orders through fulfilment, catch low stock before it sells out.
Support Staff
Internal, limited role		 Shop staff who answer calls and messages about existing orders. Look up an order by ID or phone number, read its status and history, update delivery status — without the ability to change prices or delete products.

US-S11As a shopper, I want to save items to a wishlist so I can come back when I have the money.Wishlist requires login; add/remove is one click from card or product page; the wishlist page shows current price and stock status and offers move-to-cart.
US-S12As a shopper, I want to apply a discount code so I get the advertised promotional price.Code entry validates server-side against expiry, minimum order value, usage caps, and product scope; invalid codes return a specific reason, not a generic failure.

5Feature Requirements

Thirteen feature areas. Each carries numbered requirements, a priority, and acceptance criteria.

5.1 Product Catalogue

P0

The catalogue is the store's core asset. Electronics buyers decide on specifications, so product data density matters more than visual polish.

IDRequirementPri
FR-CAT-1Categories support one level of nesting (e.g. Computers → Laptops). Each has a name, slug, optional image, and sort order.P0
FR-CAT-2Products belong to exactly one category and one brand, and carry name, slug, rich-text description, warranty term, and an active/inactive flag.P0
FR-CAT-3Products carry a structured specification set as key–value pairs (e.g. Processor: Snapdragon 8 Gen 3), rendered as a table and usable as filter input later.P0
FR-CAT-4Products support variants (colour, storage, RAM). Each variant has its own SKU, price, compare-at price, stock count, and optional image. Products with no meaningful options carry a single default variant so pricing and stock logic never branches.P0
FR-CAT-5Products support multiple images with a designated primary image and manual ordering. Images are served in a web-optimised format at listing and detail sizes.P0
FR-CAT-6Product pages show up to eight related products from the same category, excluding out-of-stock items.P1
FR-CAT-7Inactive products return 404 on the storefront and are hidden from all listings, but remain intact in historical orders.P0
FR-CAT-8A compare_at_price greater than the selling price renders as a struck-through original with a calculated discount percentage.P1

Acceptance criteria

  • Selecting a variant updates price, stock badge, SKU, and gallery without a page reload.
  • A product with zero stock across all variants renders an "Out of stock" badge and a disabled add-to-cart button.
  • Category pages include products from child categories.
  • Deleting a category with products attached is blocked with a clear message rather than cascading.

5.2 Search & Discovery

P0
IDRequirementPri
FR-SRC-1Keyword search across product name, brand name, model, SKU, and short description, backed by a MySQL FULLTEXT index with a LIKE fallback for short queries.P0
FR-SRC-2Faceted filters: category, brand, price range, in-stock only, minimum rating. Facets reflect the current result set and show counts.P0
FR-SRC-3Sort options: relevance (search only), price ascending, price descending, newest, best rated, best selling.P0
FR-SRC-4Server-side pagination, 24 items per page, with total count returned so the UI can render page controls.P0
FR-SRC-5All search, filter, sort, and page state is encoded in URL query parameters so results are shareable and survive refresh and back-navigation.P0
FR-SRC-6Search-as-you-type suggestions after three characters, debounced at 250 ms, capped at eight results.P2

Acceptance criteria

  • Searching an exact model number returns that product in the first position.
  • Applying two brands and a price range narrows correctly: brands OR'd together, then AND'd with price.
  • Copying the URL into a new tab reproduces the identical result set.
  • A zero-result search renders a suggestion state, never an empty grid.

5.3 Shopping Cart

P0
IDRequirementPri
FR-CRT-1Guests get a cart persisted in localStorage; authenticated users get a server-side cart keyed to their account.P0
FR-CRT-2On login, the guest cart merges into the server cart: quantities sum, and any line exceeding available stock is clamped with a visible notice.P0
FR-CRT-3Cart lines reference a variant, not a product, and store quantity. Line quantity is editable and removable.P0
FR-CRT-4Cart totals are always computed server-side at checkout. Client-side totals are display-only and never trusted.P0

FR-CRT-6A mini-cart in the header shows item count and subtotal and is reachable from every page.P1
Design noteAdding to cart never reserves stock. Reservation happens only when an order is confirmed (§5.11). Reserving at cart-add would let abandoned carts starve real buyers, and expiring reservations is complexity a six-week build cannot absorb.

P0

5.5 Payments

P0
IDRequirementPri
FR-PAY-1Cash on Delivery is available when the order total is at or below a configurable cap and the shipping zone permits COD.P0
FR-PAY-2Online payment via SSLCommerz hosted checkout. The customer is redirected to SSLCommerz; card and wallet credentials never reach this application.P0
FR-PAY-3Success, failure, and cancel callback URLs are implemented. The browser redirect updates the UI only — it is never the source of truth for payment state.P0
FR-PAY-4An IPN webhook endpoint receives the gateway's server-to-server notification. Payment is confirmed only after the transaction is re-validated against the gateway's validation API and the amount is checked against the stored order total.P0
FR-PAY-5Payment state transitions are idempotent. A repeated IPN for an already-confirmed order is acknowledged and ignored, never double-applied.P0
FR-PAY-6A Payment record stores gateway transaction ID, method, amount, currency, status, and the raw gateway response for reconciliation.P0
FR-PAY-7Failed or abandoned online payments leave the order in pending with stock untouched and the cart intact so the customer can retry.P0
FR-PAY-8Refunds are recorded manually by an admin — status and reference only. Gateway-initiated refunds are out of scope for v1.P1
CriticalAn order must never be marked paid on the strength of a browser redirect alone. A redirect URL is user-controllable; treating it as proof of payment is a direct route to free merchandise. Confirmation requires the server-side validation call described in FR-PAY-4.

  • Supported: current and previous major versions of Chrome, Firefox, Safari, and Edge; Chrome and Samsung Internet on Android; Safari on iOS 15+.

9.3 Accessibility

  • WCAG 2.1 AA for colour contrast on text and interactive controls.
  • All interactive elements reachable and operable by keyboard, with a visible focus ring.
  • Every form input has an associated label; errors are announced through aria-live.
  • Every product image carries meaningful alt text drawn from the product name and variant.
  • Semantic headings and landmarks throughout, not div soup.

9.4 Reliability and operations

  • Target uptime 99.5% monthly, excluding announced maintenance.
  • Automated nightly database backup with 14-day retention. A restore must be tested once before launch — an untested backup is not a backup.
  • Application errors logged with request context; a 500 rate above 1% of requests triggers an alert.
  • Database migrations are forward-only and reviewed before production application.

9.5 Maintainability

  • Business logic in services/ with unit tests for order placement, stock movement, coupon validation, and payment confirmation. These four are where money is lost when they break.

  • ruff and black for Python; ESLint and Prettier for the frontend.
  • Seed script that populates categories, brands, ten sample products with variants and images, and a test coupon — so a fresh clone is demonstrable in one command.

10Security & Compliance

This system handles money, addresses, and phone numbers. These requirements are not optional.

10.1 Authentication and session security

  • Passwords hashed with Django's default PBKDF2 hasher. Django's password validators enforce minimum length and reject common passwords.
  • Access tokens are short-lived (15 minutes). Refresh tokens rotate on use and the old token is blacklisted, so a stolen refresh token has a narrow window.
  • Refresh tokens are stored in an HttpOnly, Secure, SameSite=Lax cookie. The access token is held in memory only. Neither goes in localStorage, which is readable by any injected script.
  • Rate limits: 5 login attempts per email per 15 minutes; 3 password-reset requests per email per hour; 10 coupon validations per session per minute.
  • Password reset tokens are single-use and expire in 60 minutes.

10.2 Authorisation

  • Every admin endpoint carries an explicit DRF permission class. Hiding a button in React is not access control.
  • Object-level checks on all customer resources: a user may read only their own orders, addresses, cart, and wishlist. Enumerating another customer's order by changing an ID must return 404, not 403 — a 403 confirms the record exists.
  • Order references are non-sequential enough to resist guessing, and are still authorisation-checked on every access.
  • Staff role is enforced endpoint by endpoint, not by a single blanket check.

10.3 Payment security

  • No card data ever touches this application. SSLCommerz hosted checkout means the store never sees, transmits, or stores a card number, which keeps PCI-DSS scope minimal.
  • Payment confirmation requires the server-side validation call against the gateway's validation API. The browser redirect is display only.
  • The IPN endpoint verifies the gateway signature and re-validates the transaction before changing any order state.
  • The paid amount is compared against the stored order total. A mismatch is logged and flagged for manual review rather than auto-confirmed.
  • Order totals are always recomputed server-side at placement. A client-supplied total is never trusted, in any code path.

10.4 Application security

  • HTTPS everywhere, HSTS enabled, SECURE_SSL_REDIRECT=True in production.
  • SQL injection prevented by using the ORM. Any raw SQL uses parameterised queries — never string interpolation.
  • XSS prevented by React's default escaping. Admin-authored rich-text descriptions are sanitised on save with an allowlist, not on render.
  • CORS restricted to known frontend origins. Wildcards are not used in production.
  • Uploads validated by content type and magnitude, re-encoded through Pillow to strip embedded payloads, and served from a separate origin or path that never executes code.
  • DEBUG=False in production with a strict ALLOWED_HOSTS. Stack traces are never rendered to a user.
  • Dependencies pinned; pip-audit and npm audit run before each release.

10.5 Data handling

  • Personal data collected is limited to what fulfilment requires: name, email, phone, delivery address.
  • Order records retain address snapshots for accounting and dispute resolution even after a customer deletes a saved address.
  • Admin access to customer data is limited to the admin role; staff see only what an order requires.
  • A published privacy policy and terms of service are a launch prerequisite, not a follow-up task.
  • Database backups are encrypted at rest and access-restricted.

15.1 Order status state machine

                    ┌──────────────► cancelled ◄──────────┐
                    │                                     │
                    │                                     │
  pending ──────► confirmed ──────► packed ──────► shipped ──────► delivered
     │                │                                              │
     │                │                                              │
     └──► cancelled   └──► refunded ◄────────────────────────────────┘

  Transition rules
  ────────────────
  pending   → confirmed   COD placement, or validated online payment. Decrements stock.
  pending   → cancelled   Customer or admin. No stock change (never decremented).
  confirmed → packed      Admin only.
  confirmed → cancelled   Customer or admin. Restores stock. Releases coupon redemption.
  packed    → shipped     Admin only. Requires courier + tracking number.
  packed    → cancelled   Admin only. Restores stock.
  shipped   → delivered   Admin only. Unlocks review eligibility for the items.
  shipped   → cancelled   Admin only (failed delivery / COD refusal). Restores stock.
  delivered → refunded    Admin only. Manual record; no gateway call in v1.

  Terminal states: delivered, cancelled, refunded.
  Any transition not listed is rejected with 422 and is not logged as a state change.
